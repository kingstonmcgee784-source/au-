## 1. A hiking Fed and 5% yields set the style bias toward value and defense

**The macro regime is "no landing": strong growth, sticky inflation and a Fed turning hawkish.** On 10/28 the FOMC held at **3.75–4.00%** on a 10–2 vote, and both dissenters (Cook and Hammack) wanted a 25bp hike. The statement read "Economic activity is expanding at a strong pace… Inflation remains elevated" ([Federal Reserve](https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/files/monetary20261028a1.pdf)). Since then the data have run hot:

- **October CPI** rose **+0.4% m/m** headline and +0.3% core, both above consensus. The latest year-over-year reading is **3.7% (September)** ([BLS](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/cpi.htm)).
- **October payrolls** rose **+165k** against a consensus of about 59–65k, and unemployment fell to **4.1%** ([BLS](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/empsit.nr0.htm)).
- **Q3 real GDP** grew at a **+3.4%** annualized rate ([BEA](https://www.bea.gov/news/2026/gdp-advance-estimate-3rd-quarter-2026)).

After the CPI print, futures priced **88% odds of +25bp and 11% odds of +50bp** at the December 8–9 meeting ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/10/market-spooked-because-now-the-fed-may-need-a-supersized-hike-in-december.html)). By 11/16, secondary sources put the odds of a hike at about 94–100% ([Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/investing/2026/11/16/theres-now-100-chance-fed-rate-hike-in-december-thats-not-worst-news-for-wall-street/)). These are secondary-source estimates, not CME data. The 2-year yield jumped 17bp to 4.73% on 11/10, and the 10-year is about **5.0–5.14%** depending on the snapshot ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/currencies/articles/2-treasury-yield-just-had-202439547.html); [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/dow-p-500-nasdaq-futures-020421221.html)). Credit is calm, with high-yield spreads at about **320bp** ([Saxo](https://www.home.saxo/en-mena/content/articles/macro/market-quick-take---us-treasury-yields-rip-higher---11-november-2026-11112026)).

**Equities are cheap on earnings but nervous on rates.** The S&P 500 closed at **7,561.67** on 11/13 and is about **+10.6% YTD**. The Russell 2000 closed at about **2,790** and is about **+13.6% YTD** ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/dow-p-500-nasdaq-futures-020421221.html); [CurrentLogic](https://currentlogic.substack.com/p/dispersion-brief-week-in-review-nov-b00); [24/7 Wall St.](https://247wallst.com/investing/2026/11/13/small-caps-are-wobbling-these-dividend-stocks-are-solid-buys/)). Small caps, however, have given back gains since July, when the Russell 2000 was up about 20% YTD. Two things explain the cheap multiple:

- **Earnings outran prices.** S&P 500 Q3 EPS grew **+31.4%**, so the forward P/E has compressed to **17.7x**, the lowest since November 2023 ([FactSet](https://advantage.factset.com/hubfs/Website/Resources%20Section/Research%20Desk/Earnings%20Insight/EarningsInsight_110626A.pdf)).
- **Small caps look cheaper still**, at about **16x trailing earnings vs about 25x for the S&P** ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/10/baron-capital-small-cap-stock-picks.html)).

The S&P 500's position relative to its 200-day average is unresolved. One source puts the 200-day at 7,343.53 and another at 7,675.02, so the chart cannot be read with confidence. Monday futures pointed lower, and a live snapshot showed the S&P down about 1.3% and the VIX near 21. Treat both as provisional.

**Style favorability.** In order of preference, the regime favors:

1. Value over growth.
2. Profitable, low-leverage small caps over unprofitable or levered ones.
3. Sectors with pricing power or defensive demand (energy, health care, staples).

Royce's data show **small-cap value at +17.0% vs small-cap growth at +10.7%** YTD through September ([Royce](https://www.royceinvest.com/insights/small-cap-recap)). The AI trade has a new problem on top of its capex-return doubts. Jensen Huang **resigned as Nvidia CEO on 11/12**, the stock fell as much as 16% intraday, and FQ3 results are due **11/17** under interim CEO Colette Kress ([BNN Bloomberg](https://www.bnnbloomberg.ca/investing/2026/11/12/nvidia-sell-off-continues-after-ceo-jensen-huang-steps-down/)). Chip stocks also face fears of data-center regulation now that Democrats control Congress ([Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/fionariley/2026/11/13/ai-and-chip-stocks-finish-down-on-friday-as-markets-digest-nvidia-turmoil/)). The screen reflects this: it rewards names whose earnings do not depend on AI capex and whose balance sheets do not need cheap money.

## 7. Biggest risks: rates, Nvidia and the funding cliff

The **rate risk** is a December hike and possibly 50bp: an 11% probability on 11/10. With the 10-year above 5%, small caps get compressed and levered names are hurt most: CCSI ($453M net debt), MRCY ($232M net debt) and PMTS. The **AI contagion risk** is concentrated on **11/17**. A weak Nvidia print, or governance fallout from the CEO's exit, would hit PLAB, MRCY and the broken semis directly, and would hit crowded momentum winners such as RSKD and LIVN through de-grossing.

The **fiscal risk** comes from the stopgap that funds the government through **12/11/2026**. Three lapses have occurred since October 2025, and a debt-ceiling fight is expected in early 2027 ([Admiral Markets](https://admiralmarkets.com/analytics/traders-blog/us-government-funding-deadline-december-11-shutdown)). That matters most for defense (MRCY) and for Medicaid-exposed HCSG. The **inflation risk** is that the 12/10 CPI comes in hot a day after the FOMC decision.

## Conclusion

This tape gives the edge to a balance sheet more than to a growth rate. The names that survived combine positive estimate revisions with net cash or minimal debt, and their earnings do not depend on AI capex. The macro is making that same distinction, and it explains why a nursing-home services company and a community bank outrank every semiconductor in the screen. The practical takeaway is patience. Three of the top five are extended after earnings gaps. The best entries are likely after the 11/17 Nvidia print and around the 12/9 FOMC, when volatility could deliver the pullbacks to the 20-day and 50-day averages that turn moderate scores into favorable risk/reward. The biggest blind spot is an energy sector up 45% that this screen did not cover in small/mid caps. That should be the first follow-up, together with Form 4 and 13F pulls for the top five.
